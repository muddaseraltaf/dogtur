**The best dog food for a Yorkie is a complete-and-balanced small-breed (or Yorkie-specific) formula with tiny, easy-to-grip kibble, moderate fat and precise portions — most adult Yorkies eat roughly 140–350 calories a day, depending on weight.** For a Yorkie puppy, pick a puppy or all-life-stages small-breed food and feed it in several small meals, because very small puppies are prone to low blood sugar.

A Yorkie rarely tops seven pounds, so every decision is scaled down: the kibble has to fit a toy muzzle, crowded teeth invite dental disease, and an extra handful of treats is a big share of the day. Because the breed is long-lived, many Yorkies spend years as seniors, which is why this guide sits in our senior nutrition section and bridges to our [senior dog nutrition guide](/senior-dog-nutrition/senior-dog-nutrition-guide/).

> **How we wrote this:** this page is research-based, not tested. We have not run feeding trials. Product notes come from the manufacturers' stated features and general veterinary guidance, and every source is linked.

## Key takeaways

- Choose small, grippable kibble and an AAFCO "complete and balanced" statement for the right life stage (growth, adult maintenance or senior).
- Daily calories are tiny. Use the [feeding table below](#how-much-to-feed-a-yorkie-by-weight) as a starting point and adjust by body condition, not by the scoop.
- Puppies need frequent small meals. VCA warns that very small puppies are prone to hypoglycemia, and wobbliness or lethargy is a call-your-vet-now situation.
- Keep fat moderate. Yorkies are often described as prone to pancreatitis, so discuss very rich formulas with your vet.
- Dental disease is common in the breed. Crunch helps a little, brushing does the real work.
- Toy breeds are usually treated as seniors from about 10 years: soften textures if teeth hurt, and weigh monthly.

## What does a Yorkshire Terrier's body need from its food?

A Yorkie's food must work at toy-breed scale: calorie-dense nutrition in very small portions, kibble small enough for a tiny muzzle, dental support for crowded teeth, omega fatty acids for the coat, and enough aroma to tempt a picky eater.

The hypoglycemia point comes first because it is a real emergency risk. VCA's Yorkshire Terrier breed guide says: "Yorkie puppies should be fed often to prevent hypoglycemia, a serious condition to which very small puppies are prone." [VCA](https://vcahospitals.com/adler/know-your-pet/dog-breeds/yorkshire-terrier). Wobbliness, lethargy or disorientation in a tiny puppy means calling your vet now.

Three traits shape every food choice. Metabolism: small dogs burn plenty of calories per pound, but the total ration is so small that obesity sneaks up fast. The mouth: a full set of teeth in a toy jaw is why VCA notes that regular brushing "may prevent periodontal disease, which is common in Yorkies." The coat: long, fine hair benefits from omega-3 and omega-6 fatty acids and biotin.

## What to look for on the label

- **Small, grippable kibble.** The most practical feature. Breed-specific formulas shape the kibble for a Yorkie's muzzle, so tiny jaws can pick it up rather than push it around the bowl.
- **An AAFCO statement for the right life stage.** "Complete and balanced" for growth, adult maintenance or senior. A breed name on the bag is not a substitute. For general label-reading help, the [WSAVA Global Nutrition Guidelines](https://wsava.org/global-guidelines/global-nutrition-guidelines/) hub points owners to a "Selecting a Pet Food" tool and warns that an ingredient list says nothing about ingredient quality.
- **Dental support.** Sodium tripolyphosphate can help reduce tartar and crunch encourages chewing. Neither replaces brushing and professional cleanings.
- **Omega-3, omega-6 and biotin.** Support skin and coat from the inside.
- **Moderate fat, controlled calories.** Discuss very high-fat formulas with your vet.
- **High palatability.** A perfect formula your Yorkie won't eat is worthless, and skipped meals are riskier in a toy breed.

## What to feed a Yorkie puppy

A Yorkie puppy needs a food labeled for growth (puppy or all-life-stages) in the smallest kibble available. Feed small meals often and never let a tiny puppy go many hours without food. Many owners use three to four meals a day; ask your vet for a schedule that fits your puppy, since VCA's guidance is simply "fed often."

- **Keep the food consistent.** Switch foods slowly over 7 to 10 days, one change at a time.
- **Moisten if needed.** A little warm water on puppy kibble helps a small mouth and adds aroma. Soaked kibble is also a good option when teething makes hard pieces unpleasant.
- **Know the warning signs.** Weakness, wobbling, tremors or glassy eyes mean call your vet immediately.
- **Move to adult food** at roughly 10 to 12 months, gradually, and start tooth-brushing in the puppy year.

Puppy calorie targets are in the table below. The rule of thumb for puppies 4 months and older is a multiplier of 2.0 (younger puppies use 3.0), so recalculate every couple of weeks as your puppy grows.

## How much to feed a Yorkie by weight

Most Yorkies eat far less than owners expect. The table shows estimated calories per day, computed with the resting energy requirement formula: **RER = 70 × (weight in kg)^0.75**, multiplied by a life-stage factor (1.6 for a neutered adult, 2.0 for a puppy 4 months or older), as published by Jennifer Coates, DVM, in [PetMD's calorie guide](https://www.petmd.com/dog/nutrition/how-many-calories-does-a-dog-need). Pounds are divided by 2.2 to get kilograms, and results are rounded to the nearest 5.

| Body weight | RER (resting) | Neutered adult (×1.6) | Puppy 4 months+ (×2.0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 lb (1.4 kg) | 90 kcal | 140 kcal | 175 kcal |
| 5 lb (2.3 kg) | 130 kcal | 205 kcal | 260 kcal |
| 7 lb (3.2 kg) | 165 kcal | 265 kcal | 335 kcal |
| 10 lb (4.5 kg) | 220 kcal | 350 kcal | 435 kcal |

Worked example: a 5 lb neutered adult is 2.27 kg. 2.27^0.75 is about 1.85, times 70 is 129 kcal (RER), times 1.6 is about 207 kcal a day.

**Cups depend on your food's calorie density.** The bag shows kilocalories per cup, and it varies a lot between brands. Divide the daily calories by that number: a 5 lb adult on a food with 400 kcal per cup needs about half a cup a day, split into two or three meals. Our [dog calorie calculator](/tools/dog-calorie-calculator/) does this for you. These are estimates: intact, very active, underweight or senior dogs differ, and treats count. Adjust by body condition and your vet's advice. For why even small overfeeding matters, VCA advises restricting calories if a Yorkie gains too much weight.

## Foods Yorkies can eat (and can't)

Always check portion size first: a bite that is nothing to a Lab is a meal to a five-pound dog. Anything beyond the daily calorie figure above counts as extra, and treats are best kept under about a tenth of the day's calories (a common vet guideline, not from the sources below).

**Generally fine in small amounts** (per the AKC guides to [human foods](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/nutrition/human-foods-dogs-can-and-cant-eat/) and [fruits and vegetables](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/nutrition/fruits-vegetables-dogs-can-and-cant-eat/)):

| Food | Notes for a Yorkie |
|---|---|
| Eggs | Fully cooked, with no salt, butter or seasoning. AKC advises against feeding only raw egg whites. Fine as a breakfast topper in a tiny portion. |
| Plain turkey meat | AKC advises removing excess fat and skin and all bones. |
| Cheese | Small amounts. AKC suggests lower-fat types such as cottage cheese; some dogs digest dairy poorly. |
| Peanut butter | An occasional treat. AKC says about a teaspoon or less for dogs generally, so give a small fraction of that to a Yorkie, and check the label for xylitol. |
| Carrots, green beans (plain), pumpkin (100% puree), blueberries, strawberries, watermelon (no rind or seeds), apples (no seeds or core) | AKC lists these with caveats: plain and unsalted, moderate amounts, sugary fruit as an occasional treat. |

**Avoid** (per the [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/animal-poison-control/people-foods-avoid-feeding-your-pets) and AKC):

| Food | Why |
|---|---|
| Xylitol (sugar-free gum, some peanut butters and baked goods) | [VCA](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/xylitol-toxicity) calls it extremely toxic. It causes low blood sugar and, at higher doses, liver failure. |
| Grapes and raisins | ASPCA: may cause kidney damage. |
| Onions, garlic, chives | May damage red blood cells and cause anemia. AKC says garlic is more toxic than onion. |
| Chocolate and caffeine | Methylxanthines can cause tremors, seizures and abnormal heart rhythm. |
| Macadamia nuts | Weakness, tremors, vomiting and fever, usually within 12 hours per ASPCA. |
| Alcohol and raw yeast dough | Intoxication; dough can swell in the stomach. |
| Cooked bones | AKC warns cooked poultry and pork bones splinter. Choking and blockage risk is higher in a toy mouth. |
| Avocado | AKC: flesh isn't toxic but is high in fat, and the pit is a blockage hazard. |
| High-fat scraps, fatty nuts | ASPCA notes high-fat nuts can cause vomiting, diarrhea and possibly pancreatitis. |
| Heavily salted foods | ASPCA: excess salt can cause thirst, urination and, in severe cases, tremors and seizures. |

If your Yorkie eats anything on the avoid list, call your vet or the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at (888) 426-4435 right away.

## Dry or wet food for Yorkies?

Dry kibble is the usual default because the chewing action helps a little with tartar. Wet food suits seniors with tooth loss, sore mouths and picky eaters, and many owners top kibble with a spoonful of wet. If you feed mostly wet food, step up brushing and keep dental checkups on schedule, since soft food does less for the teeth. Whichever you choose, match the calories on the label to the table above.

## Feeding a Yorkie with dental disease

Many middle-aged Yorkies have had extractions, sore gums or professional cleanings. Kibble's chewing action gives mild cleaning, but only if the dog can chew comfortably:

- **Soften or mix.** Soak the usual kibble in warm water for 10 to 15 minutes, or mix a spoonful of matching wet food through it.
- **Go smaller, not just softer.** Dental pain often makes large pieces the first thing a dog refuses.
- **Compensate on hygiene.** Softer food does less against tartar, so brush more and keep cleanings on schedule.
- **Treat appetite changes as a signal.** A Yorkie that drops kibble, chews on one side or suddenly only eats soft food may have tooth pain. That is a vet visit, not a food switch.

## Feeding the senior Yorkie

Toy breeds are typically considered senior around 10 years, and Yorkies commonly live well beyond that:

- **Calories drop; nutrient density matters more.** Metabolism slows but the need for quality protein stays high.
- **Chewing gets harder.** The soaking and mixing strategies above apply.
- **Appetite gets pickier.** Warming food slightly releases aroma, but sustained appetite loss deserves a vet check.
- **Weigh monthly.** At four pounds, a few ounces is proportionally huge. A kitchen scale and a monthly weigh-in catch problems early.

## Pickiness, pancreatitis and gulpers

- **Picky eaters.** Yorkies are notorious for training their owners. Rule out dental pain or illness with your vet first, then try consistent mealtimes and a 15-minute "eat or it waits" rule, not a rotating buffet.
- **Fat and pancreatitis.** Yorkies are often described as prone to pancreatitis, and rich, fatty foods are a common trigger. If your dog has had pancreatitis, ask your vet about a low-fat or prescription diet. This is a "discuss with your vet" point, not a diagnosis.
- **Gulpers.** A Yorkie that inhales food and then coughs or gags needs a slower pace. A slow-feeder bowl or a snuffle mat helps, and a harness rather than a collar is standard advice for toy breeds with airway concerns. Mention coughing or gagging to your vet.
- **Oversized kibble and hard treats** are a choking risk for a toy mouth.

## FAQ

### What is the best dog food for Yorkies?

There is no single best food. A complete-and-balanced small-breed or Yorkie-specific formula with tiny kibble, moderate fat and the right life-stage statement is the safest starting point. Royal Canin Yorkshire Terrier Adult and Hill's Science Diet Small & Mini Adult are the two we cover first. Confirm with your vet.

### What is the best food to feed a Yorkie puppy?

A puppy or all-life-stages small-breed food in small kibble, fed in several small meals a day so blood sugar doesn't drop. Weigh your puppy regularly and use the table above to estimate calories. Wobbliness or lethargy is an emergency.

### How much should I feed my Yorkie?

An adult neutered Yorkie of 5 lb needs roughly 205 calories a day by the standard formula; a 7 lb dog about 265. Convert that to cups using the kcal per cup on your bag, or use our [calorie calculator](/tools/dog-calorie-calculator/). Adjust by body condition.

### What is the best kibble for Yorkies?

The best kibble is the one with the smallest pieces your Yorkie can pick up easily, the right life-stage statement and moderate fat. Breed-specific kibble is shaped for the Yorkie jaw, though a few owners of very tiny Yorkies find even that slightly large.

### Is dry or wet food better for Yorkies?

Dry food is the better default for most adults because of the chewing action. Wet food suits seniors with tooth loss and picky eaters. Many owners use kibble with a spoonful of wet food on top.

### Do Yorkies need grain-free food?

No. Nothing about the breed requires it, and grain-inclusive formulas are the conservative choice. Go grain-free only on your vet's advice.

### When should my Yorkie switch to senior food?

Toy breeds are typically considered senior around 10 years. Switch on signs, such as slowing metabolism, weight changes or chewing difficulty, rather than a birthday. Our [senior dog food guide](/senior-dog-nutrition/best-dog-food-senior-dogs/) walks through the transition.

### Why does my Yorkie throw up yellow bile in the morning?

Small dogs fed infrequently sometimes vomit bile on an empty stomach. A small late-evening snack or three meals can help, but recurring vomiting deserves a vet visit.

### What if my Yorkie refuses every food I try?

Rule out dental pain or illness first. Then try a highly palatable small-breed formula, warm it slightly, keep mealtimes consistent and resist adding toppers. Persistent refusal in a toy breed needs a prompt vet visit.

## The bottom line

For a Yorkie, kibble size is the foundation. Start with a small-breed or breed-specific formula, keep fat moderate and portions precise, support the teeth with crunch and real brushing, and tempt the picky ones with aroma. This breed routinely reaches its teens, so the habits you build now compound for a decade or more.
