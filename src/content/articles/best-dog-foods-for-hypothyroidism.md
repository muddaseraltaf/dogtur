The best diet for a dog with hypothyroidism is a measured, high-protein, higher-fiber weight-management food fed to the dog's target weight, alongside daily thyroid hormone from your vet. No food treats hypothyroidism; levothyroxine does. Diet's real jobs are controlling the weight gain the condition causes, supporting the coat, and keeping meals and the pill on a consistent routine.

This guide is built on [Cornell's veterinary college](https://vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/riney-canine-health-center/canine-health-information/hypothyroidism), the [MSD Veterinary Manual](https://www.msdvetmanual.com/endocrine-system/the-thyroid-gland/hypothyroidism-in-animals), and our [senior dog nutrition guide](/senior-dog-nutrition/senior-dog-nutrition-guide/). It is research-based, not tested, and it gives no medicine doses: those come from your vet.

## Key takeaways

- No food treats hypothyroidism. Daily levothyroxine, prescribed and monitored by your vet, does.
- Feed for the target weight: worked calorie example below (RER at target weight, 70 × kg^0.75), measured portions, treats counted.
- Worth discussing with your vet: Royal Canin Satiety Support (veterinary diet), Hill's Science Diet Adult Perfect Weight, Purina Pro Plan Weight Management, Wellness CORE Healthy Weight.
- Timing: follow your vet on pill-and-meal timing and keep it identical every day.
- Skip kelp and "thyroid-boosting" blends unless your vet approves; low-iodine diets and avoiding soy are not needed for most dogs.

## Can food treat hypothyroidism in dogs?

No. Hypothyroidism is treated with levothyroxine, a synthetic thyroid hormone your veterinarian prescribes and monitors with bloodwork for the rest of your dog's life. Food cannot restart thyroid function or replace the medication — any product or supplement claiming to "boost the thyroid" or manage hypothyroidism without medication should be treated with deep skepticism.

Most cases of canine hypothyroidism are primary in nature — caused by the immune system destroying the thyroid gland — and the [MSD Veterinary Manual](https://www.msdvetmanual.com/endocrine-system/the-thyroid-gland/hypothyroidism-in-animals?query=HYPOTHYROIDISM%20in%20dog) names levothyroxine the thyroid hormone replacement compound of choice in dogs. Replacement therapy is, with few exceptions, lifelong. [Cornell](https://vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/riney-canine-health-center/canine-health-information/hypothyroidism) notes it is usually given twice daily to start, with a vet recheck and blood tests a few weeks after starting, then periodic monitoring for life. The good news: with accurate diagnosis and monitoring, the prognosis is good — energy typically returns within weeks.

Two rules that matter more than any food choice: give the medication exactly as your vet directs, and never adjust the dose yourself. Persistent symptoms despite treatment are a recheck conversation, not a diet experiment.

## Symptom checklist to discuss with your vet

These signs overlap heavily with normal aging, so "he's just getting old" is the assumption to avoid. [Cornell](https://vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/riney-canine-health-center/canine-health-information/hypothyroidism) lists the most common signs as lethargy and reduced activity, weight gain, cold intolerance, and skin and coat changes. Tick what you see and bring the list to your vet:

- [ ] Sleeping more, less interested in walks or play
- [ ] Weight gain without eating more
- [ ] Seeks warm spots or seems to feel cold
- [ ] Thinning or lost hair that is not itchy (trunk, tail base, bridge of the nose)
- [ ] Dry, dull, brittle coat; slow regrowth after clipping
- [ ] Darkened skin
- [ ] Recurrent skin or ear infections
- [ ] Less common: digestive upset, weakness, a slow heart rate (a vet finds this)

Diagnosis needs bloodwork read alongside the signs; Cornell lists total T4, free T4, TSH and thyroid autoantibody tests. Breeds Cornell lists among the most commonly diagnosed include Golden Retrievers, Boxers, Beagles, Cocker Spaniels and English Setters. Cushing's disease can look similar, so see [Cushing's disease in senior dogs](/senior-dog-health/cushings-disease-in-senior-dogs/), and our [senior health guide](/senior-dog-health/senior-dog-health-guide/) for context.

## The real nutritional job: weight-loss calorie math

Hypothyroidism slows metabolism, so the amount that used to maintain weight slowly adds pounds. Weight gain without increased appetite is a classic sign, and the [MSD Veterinary Manual](https://www.msdvetmanual.com/endocrine-system/the-thyroid-gland/hypothyroidism-in-animals) notes obesity develops in some dogs and that weight can take up to 3 months to improve on treatment.

**How to calculate a starting calorie target.** Resting energy requirement (RER) is 70 × (weight in kg)^0.75. For weight loss, calculate it from your vet's target weight, then multiply by a factor. The worked example in [Today's Veterinary Nurse](https://todaysveterinarynurse.com/nutrition/veterinary-nutrition-math/) uses a factor of 1 for a weight-loss plan. Some programs start lower, at about 80% of RER, so your vet may choose a different number. Treat this as a starting point to adjust with weekly or biweekly weigh-ins.

**Worked example: a dog who weighs 62 lb and should weigh 50 lb.**

1. Target weight: 50 lb ÷ 2.2046 = 22.7 kg
2. 22.7^0.75 = 10.4
3. RER = 70 × 10.4 = **about 727 kcal/day** (factor 1.0)
4. Treats at up to 10% of calories: about 70 kcal, which leaves about 655 kcal for meals
5. If the food is 340 kcal per cup, 655 ÷ 340 = **about 1.9 cups/day**, split into two measured meals (always use your own food's kcal per cup from the bag)
6. Weigh every two weeks. If weight is not dropping, recheck with your vet before cutting further.

The commonly cited safe pace is about 1 to 2% of body weight per week, which for this dog is roughly 0.6 to 1.2 lb a week, or 10 to 20 weeks to lose 12 lb. Ask your vet for the pace they want. To run your own numbers, use our [dog calorie calculator](/tools/dog-calorie-calculator/).

**Other food rules that matter more than the brand:**

- **High protein, higher fiber.** Protein preserves muscle during weight loss and fiber helps fullness. See our [high-fiber food guide](/senior-dog-nutrition/best-high-fiber-dog-food/).
- **Measure everything**, and count treats in the daily total.
- **Reassess after medication starts.** Calorie needs often rise once metabolism normalizes; your vet adjusts at recheck bloodwork.

## Myths: iodine, kelp, soy and "thyroid diets"

| Myth | What we found | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "Feed a low-iodine food" | No veterinary page we opened recommends it. Complete and balanced foods are formulated to include iodine, and iodine problems in the case literature involve unbalanced diets, so use a complete food and check homemade diets with a veterinary nutritionist. | [Published case report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9234484/) (dog with diet-induced iodine deficiency; we could not open the full text) |
| "Kelp or iodine supplements boost the thyroid" | No evidence we found that they treat canine hypothyroidism. Kelp products vary in iodine content and are not regulated like drugs. Ask your vet before adding anything. | None found for benefit; ask your vet |
| "Soy causes hypothyroidism" | The human ATA treatment guideline says soy can impair levothyroxine absorption, not that it causes hypothyroidism. We found no dog-specific study. Mayo says people with hypothyroidism do not need to avoid soy completely. | [ATA guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4267409/); [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/hypothyroidism/expert-answers/hyperthyroidism/faq-20058188) (human) |
| "Avoid broccoli, kale and other goitrogens" | Not a veterinary recommendation we could find for normal dog-food amounts. | None found |
| "A grain-free or raw diet fixes thyroid disease" | No. The thyroid condition does not dictate grain-free vs grain-inclusive; legume-heavy grain-free diets remain part of a DCM discussion worth raising with your vet. | See our [senior nutrition guide](/senior-dog-nutrition/senior-dog-nutrition-guide/) |

## Timing food and levothyroxine

Vet sources differ slightly, and all stress consistency. Do not change anything without asking your vet, and do not adjust the dose yourself.

| Source | What it says |
|---|---|
| [MSD Veterinary Manual](https://www.msdvetmanual.com/endocrine-system/the-thyroid-gland/hypothyroidism-in-animals) | In dogs, levothyroxine is given orally on an empty stomach; if food must be given, do the same on blood-sample days |
| [VCA](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/levothyroxine) | Can be given with or without food, but the same way every day |
| [AKC](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/health/levothyroxine-for-dogs/) | Generally absorbed best on an empty stomach; if that is not possible, consistency matters most |

VCA also lists oral antacids, iron and high amounts of fiber as things to use cautiously with levothyroxine. Tell your vet about every supplement and high-fiber treat. That matters here, because weight-loss plans often add fiber. A missed dose is a question for your vet or the label.

## What to look for on the label

- **A weight-management formula** with high protein, controlled fat and added fiber.
- **L-carnitine**, common in weight-control foods. Supportive, not a treatment.
- **Omega-3 and omega-6 fatty acids** for skin and coat. [Cornell](https://vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/riney-canine-health-center/canine-health-information/hypothyroidism) says skin and haircoat improvement is the slowest change and may take several months, so do not judge a food in two weeks.
- **A complete-and-balanced (AAFCO) statement.** "Thyroid-specific" marketing adds nothing veterinary.
- **Calories per cup or per can**, so you can use the math above.

## Treats, routine, and tracking progress

Most weight-loss plans fail on treats, not meals. Keep treats to about 10% of daily calories, feed two measured meals rather than free-feeding, weigh monthly on the same scale, and log weight, energy and coat. Bring your exact feeding amounts to every recheck: if weight keeps climbing despite careful feeding, that is a conversation for your vet, who decides based on bloodwork, not how the dog looks. ## FAQ

### Should I look for a low-iodine dog food?

No — this is one of the most common pieces of bad advice on this topic. Complete-and-balanced foods are formulated to include iodine, and we found no veterinary source recommending a low-iodine diet. Be cautious with kelp and "thyroid support" products, and ask your vet first.

### Can I add kelp or a thyroid-support supplement?

Ask your vet first — and expect a cautious answer. Kelp products vary in iodine, herbal thyroid blends are unproven in dogs, and supplements given near pill time may interfere with absorption. We found no evidence they help.

### Does soy in dog food worsen hypothyroidism?

In humans, soy is reported to impair levothyroxine absorption but not to cause hypothyroidism; we found no dog-specific study. Keep the medication routine consistent as your vet directs rather than hunting for a soy-free diet.

### Do I need a prescription diet for my hypothyroid dog?

Not necessarily. An over-the-counter weight-management formula with high protein, higher fiber, and L-carnitine covers most cases. A veterinary weight-loss diet like Royal Canin Satiety Support is worth discussing with your vet when weight won't budge on an OTC food, or when significant loss is needed.

### Why is my dog still gaining weight on medication?

Possibly portions and treats have drifted, but check with your vet first: persistent weight gain can mean the dose needs adjustment, or that calorie needs have changed. Bring your exact feeding amounts (food plus treats) to the recheck — dose changes are based on bloodwork, never adjusted at home.

### How many calories should a hypothyroid dog eat?
Start from RER at the target weight: 70 × kg^0.75, then your vet picks the factor. For a 50 lb (22.7 kg) target that is about 727 kcal/day at a factor of 1.0. See the [worked example](#the-real-nutritional-job-weight-loss-calorie-math).

### Should I give levothyroxine with food or on an empty stomach?
Sources differ: MSD says empty stomach, VCA says either way if consistent, AKC says empty stomach is generally best. Follow your vet and keep it identical every day.

## The bottom line

Medication treats hypothyroidism; food manages its consequences. Calculate calories for the target weight, measure portions, keep pill and meal timing consistent, and skip the "thyroid-boosting" gimmicks. Keep every recheck, and most hypothyroid dogs do very well.
