**The best dog food for a Boston Terrier is a highly digestible, small-bite formula with named animal protein and controlled calories — paired with a slow-feeder bowl, because much of the breed's gas is swallowed air rather than food.** For skin and itching, pick a complete diet your vet agrees with and use an elimination trial, not brand-hopping, to test for allergies.

Boston Terriers are 10 to 25 pounds of flat-faced charm (VCA lists 10–25 lb), and they have five feeding questions owners keep asking: gas, skin and allergies, weight, puppy food and senior food. This guide covers all five. It sits inside our [senior dog nutrition pillar](/senior-dog-nutrition/senior-dog-nutrition-guide/), because Boston Terriers typically hit their senior years around 8 to 10, and everything here applies from puppyhood to gray-muzzle days.

> **How we wrote this:** this page is research-based. We have not run feeding trials. Product notes come from label and specification analysis, and every owner experience quoted is attributed to its source.

## Key takeaways

- Choose small-bite kibble and a "complete and balanced" statement for the right life stage. Short jaws and crowded teeth make large, hard kibble awkward.
- Gas has two sources: swallowed air from the flat face (aerophagia) and fermentation of poorly digested food. A slow-feeder bowl matters as much as the food.
- Skin and itching: food allergies exist but are less common than marketing suggests. Only a vet-supervised elimination diet confirms one.
- Weight: Bostons "tend to stay in good weight or tend to be slightly overweight" (VCA), and extra pounds hurt breathing and joints. Use the [feeding table below](#how-much-to-feed-a-boston-terrier-by-weight).
- Puppies do best on a puppy formula in three to four meals a day; seniors need fewer calories but the same digestibility.
- Sudden, severe gas with vomiting, bloating, pain or appetite loss is a vet emergency, not "Boston gas."

## What a Boston Terrier's food must do

A Boston's food should be digestible, sized for a short jaw, and portioned for a breed that drifts overweight. In practice that means:

- **Named animal protein** and moderate, not high, fiber with prebiotic support.
- **Small-bite kibble.** Brachycephalic skulls leave less room for teeth, so crowded teeth are common, and large hard kibble encourages gulping. Small-breed formulas sized for dogs under about 25 pounds let a Boston chew deliberately.
- **A life-stage statement** (growth, adult maintenance, senior or all life stages). For general label-reading help, see the [WSAVA Global Nutrition Guidelines](https://wsava.org/global-guidelines/global-nutrition-guidelines/), which warns that an ingredient list alone says nothing about ingredient quality.
- **Controlled calories** from a measured daily ration (table below).
- **High digestibility.** The [MSD Veterinary Manual](https://www.msdvetmanual.com/management-and-nutrition/nutrition-small-animals/nutritional-requirements-of-small-animals) notes that dogs digest properly cooked starches at over 90% efficiency, and ties flatulence to poorly digestible carbohydrates.

## How much to feed a Boston Terrier by weight

Estimated calories per day, computed with the resting energy requirement formula **RER = 70 × (weight in kg)^0.75**, times a life-stage factor (1.6 neutered adult, 2.0 puppy 4 months or older), as published by Jennifer Coates, DVM, in [PetMD's calorie guide](https://www.petmd.com/dog/nutrition/how-many-calories-does-a-dog-need). Pounds are divided by 2.2 to get kilograms, and results are rounded to the nearest 5.

| Body weight | RER (resting) | Neutered adult (×1.6) | Puppy 4 months+ (×2.0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 lb (5.4 kg) | 250 kcal | 400 kcal | 500 kcal |
| 15 lb (6.8 kg) | 295 kcal | 470 kcal | 590 kcal |
| 20 lb (9.1 kg) | 365 kcal | 585 kcal | 730 kcal |
| 25 lb (11.3 kg) | 435 kcal | 690 kcal | 865 kcal |

Worked example: a 20 lb neutered adult is 9.07 kg. 9.07^0.75 is about 5.23, times 70 is 366 kcal (RER), times 1.6 is about 585 kcal a day.

**Cups depend on the food.** The bag lists kilocalories per cup, and it varies widely. Divide the daily calories by that number: a 20 lb adult on a food with 380 kcal per cup needs about 1.5 cups a day, split into two meals (this is an example, not a recommendation). Our [dog calorie calculator](/tools/dog-calorie-calculator/) does the maths for you. For an overweight dog, feed for the target weight and ask your vet to set it. Intact, very active and senior dogs differ, and treats count toward the total.

## Weight: the quiet Boston Terrier problem

VCA says Bostons tend to stay in good weight "or tend to be slightly overweight" and advises restricting calories if a dog gains too much. For a flat-faced breed, every extra pound makes breathing harder and stresses joints. Feed for ideal weight, measure with a scale or proper cup, and count treats as part of the day's ration, because for a food-motivated 20-pound Boston they add up fast. ## Skin, itching and food allergies

Boston Terriers are widely described as allergy-prone, and food can be a suspect when itching, ear infections and chronic digestive upset cluster together. But the evidence is more modest than the marketing.

- **Grain is not the usual culprit.** A critically appraised review in [BMC Veterinary Research](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12917-016-0633-8) of common food allergen sources found that beef and dairy products are the most commonly recognized triggers in dogs, with chicken and wheat also reported, not "fillers" in general.
- **Only an elimination trial confirms it.** The [Merck Veterinary Manual](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/integumentary-system/food-allergy/cutaneous-food-allergy-in-animals) says the only reliable way to prove a food allergy is a dietary elimination trial followed by a controlled challenge. A blood test or random brand-switching does not do it.
- **What to feed meanwhile.** If your vet confirms a trigger, a limited-ingredient or single-protein food makes the ingredient list manageable. Our [allergy guide](/senior-dog-nutrition/best-dog-food-for-allergies-and-yeast-infection/) explains how vets investigate properly.
- **Skin signs are a vet question.** Persistent itching, hot spots, ear infections or hair loss deserve an exam. Omega-3 fatty acids in a quality food support skin, but they don't treat disease.

## Why are Boston Terriers so gassy?

Boston Terriers swallow more air than most dogs. Their short, flat (brachycephalic) faces and gulpy eating style mean air goes down with every meal. A [2019 study in the Journal of Veterinary Internal Medicine](https://academic.oup.com/jvim/article/33/5/2183/8447899) notes that "excessive flatulence as a consequence of aerophagia" is often present in brachycephalic dogs. [WebMD's overview of canine flatulence](https://www.webmd.com/pets/dogs/flatulence-dogs) names Boston Terriers among short-nosed breeds that "tend to swallow a lot of air" and notes that fast eaters swallow more.

That changes the strategy. A digestible food reduces fermentable leftovers, and slower eating reduces swallowed air, so use both:

- **Use a slow-feeder bowl.** Maze and puzzle bowls force deliberate eating. Some designs work better than others, so ask your vet which suits your dog.
- **Split into two or three meals.** Smaller meals are easier to digest and reduce frantic gulping.
- **Keep the feeding area calm.** A hyped-up Boston inhales air before the first bite.
- **Rest after meals.** Skip vigorous exercise right after eating.
- **Watch gas-prone ingredients.** Vets commonly point to soy, peas and beans (poorly digestible plant sugars), dairy, very rich or fatty formulas and abrupt diet changes. "Grain-free" does not guarantee less gas, since many grain-free recipes lean on peas. Transition any new food over 7 to 10 days. Our [less-poop guide](/senior-dog-nutrition/best-dog-food-for-less-poop/) covers digestibility in depth.

**A safety note:** if gas is sudden, severe, or arrives with vomiting, bloating, abdominal pain or appetite loss, see your vet promptly. Bloating can be an emergency.

## Boston Terrier puppies

Puppyhood sets a Boston's digestive habits. Feed a growth (puppy or all-life-stages) formula with named animal protein and moderate fat, split into three to four small meals a day. Introduce the slow-feeder bowl early so deliberate eating is the default. Add new foods and treats one at a time so you can spot what disagrees with your puppy. Puppy calories are in the table above (use the 4 months+ column once your puppy is past 4 months; younger puppies use a higher factor of 3.0, so ask your vet).

## Senior Boston Terriers

Boston Terriers are typically considered senior around 8 to 10 years. Aging means fewer calories for a slower metabolism but the same easy digestibility, plus softer or smaller kibble for crowded, aging teeth. Kidney and joint support move up the priority list, and fat moves down. Our [senior dog food guide](/senior-dog-nutrition/best-dog-food-senior-dogs/) compares formulas for aging bodies. Weigh monthly and discuss any appetite or weight change with your vet.

## Health signs to discuss with your vet

No food prevents these, but a breed guide should name them. Cataracts, corneal ulcers and cherry eye are commonly reported in the breed, so report squinting, cloudiness or discharge promptly. Slipping kneecaps (patellar luxation) are also described in the breed, and excess weight worsens joint and airway problems. Keeping a Boston lean is joint and breathing care at once.

## FAQ

### What is the best dog food for a Boston Terrier?

There is no single best food. Choose a complete-and-balanced small-bite formula for the life stage, with named animal protein, moderate fat and good digestibility, then check how your dog does. Purina Pro Plan Sensitive Skin & Stomach and Hill's Sensitive Stomach & Skin are common starting points.

### What food is best for a Boston Terrier with a sensitive stomach?

A digestibility-focused formula with prebiotic fiber, probiotics and named animal protein, transitioned over 7 to 10 days. If sensitivity persists beyond a few weeks on a quality food, talk to your vet instead of cycling brands.

### What is the best kibble for a Boston Terrier?

Small-bite kibble sized for a short jaw, in a formula your dog digests well. Large hard pieces encourage gulping.

### How do I stop my Boston Terrier's gas?

Attack both sources: a digestible food, plus a slow-feeder bowl and split meals for less swallowed air. Persistent or worsening gas deserves a vet visit.

### Are Boston Terriers prone to food allergies?

The breed is described as allergy-prone, but a food allergy is confirmed only by a vet-supervised elimination diet trial. Beef and dairy are the most commonly recognized triggers in dogs, not grains as a category.

### Should I feed my Boston Terrier grain-free?

Only if your vet identifies a grain as the trigger, which is uncommon. Many grain-free recipes rely on peas or legumes, which vets list among gas-increasing ingredients.

### How much should I feed my Boston Terrier?

A neutered 15 lb adult needs roughly 470 calories a day by the standard formula, and a 20 lb adult roughly 585. Convert to cups using the kcal per cup on the bag, or use our [calorie calculator](/tools/dog-calorie-calculator/). Adjust by body condition and your vet's advice.

### Is it normal for Boston Terriers to have soft stools?

Occasional soft stools happen, but chronically soft stools mean the food isn't agreeing or something else is going on. Try a more digestible formula first; involve your vet if it persists.

### Can probiotics help my Boston Terrier's gas?

They can help when gas comes from an imbalanced gut, but they won't fix gas from swallowed air. If a quality food with probiotics plus slower eating doesn't help within a few weeks, check with your vet.

## The bottom line

For a Boston Terrier, a digestible small-bite food plus a slow-feeder bowl cuts gas, a measured ration protects the waistline, and your vet handles anything food can't fix, such as confirmed allergies or eye and joint problems.
